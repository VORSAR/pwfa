# Error correction and whole-word recall

The first PWFA library contains four 16-bit words. Each evaluation cue supplies twelve correct bits, two flipped bits and two visible omissions. The retriever sees the presented bits and the omissions; only the evaluator knows which presented bits were flipped. At $t=40$ all sixteen answers for one cue must match its original word. [Frozen library and experiment](../experiments/first-trained-recall.md) · [Strength sweep](../experiments/structural-coupling-at-retrieval.md#intermediate-strength-results)

## One cue explains the obstacle

| Cue 004 at quarter structural strength | Sixteen positions, left to right |
|---|---|
| Original stored word, known to the scorer | `1100101100001110` |
| Visible cue given to the encoder | `11001111?10011?0` |
| PWFA’s answer at time 40 | `1100101100001100` |

PWFA reversed both flips at positions 6 and 10, kept all twelve initially correct bits and recovered the omitted 0 at position 9. It returned 0 for the omitted 1 at position 15. That is **15/16 correct bits and one missed word**. Across all sixteen cues the same setting corrected 16/32 flips, filled 18/32 omissions correctly and lost 29/192 initially correct bits. Those totals do not say which repairs co-occurred. Exact recall counts their intersection *within each cue*. An oscillator may change sign several times along the way; the specified readout is at time 40. ## What the mathematics says

This is a targeted review of primary work on mixed errors and erasures, constraint-based decoding, associative-memory basins and phase-oscillator retrieval, with a source-level check of related Lean projects on 2026-11-27.

**1. Whole-word decoding uses redundancy.** Treat the four stored words as the allowed messages, or *codebook*. Hamming distance counts how many bits differ between two words. An erasure tells the decoder where a value is missing; a flipped bit looks as trustworthy as a correct bit when both are presented with equal confidence. If every two allowed words differ in at least $d_{\min}$ positions, nearest-word decoding on visible coordinates guarantees exact recovery whenever $2e+s<d_{\min}$ for at most $e$ flips and $s$ erasures. Erasing $s$ positions can hide $s$ distinctions. Each remaining wrong bit can both increase the distance to the target and reduce the distance to a competitor. For two flips and two erasures the sufficient distance is seven. This reasoning applies to arbitrary finite codebooks, including a library with no added parity bits. [Hamming (1950), geometric distance argument](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1950.tb00463.x) · [Sudan, mixed error/erasure condition](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/coding/ibm/lect5x4.pdf)

**2. A bit can be corrected using evidence from the other positions.** In a binary linear code valid words satisfy parity constraints. The pattern of failed checks, called the *syndrome*, carries information about the errors. Sipser and Spielman constructed codes in which a sufficiently small error set can be corrected by local updates on a suitably expanding graph. Chaudhuri and Fiete adapted this principle to an associative memory with a separate population of constraint units. Its strong guarantees concern networks and memories that satisfy those construction assumptions. PWFA’s four arbitrary words and its dense signed alignment matrix have no specified parity-check graph. The transferable idea is to measure how strongly the other sites support *changing this particular bit*. A global increase in coupling strength supplies no such guarantee. [Sipser & Spielman (1996)](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/PAPERS/expandersIT.pdf) · [Chaudhuri & Fiete (2019)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/97008ea27052082be055447be9e85612-Paper.pdf)

**3. Reliability can improve decoding if it carries information.** Forney’s generalized minimum-distance decoding uses reliability information to try erasures at unreliable positions. In the sum-product algorithm, a message sent along an edge uses evidence from the other incoming edges; on a cycle-free factor graph this gives exact marginals under its probability model. For PWFA, the magnitude of an origin-relative phase score is a candidate confidence measure. Its reliability on wrong bits must be measured on separate data. Our ±90° encoder initially presents a flipped bit with the same magnitude as a correct bit, so its own initial magnitude gives no warning. The evaluator’s flip labels must stay out of the decoder. A decoder that minimizes expected bit errors and one that maximizes the probability of the entire word can also make different decisions. [Forney (1966)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1966.1053873) · [Kschischang, Frey & Loeliger (2001)](https://www.cise.ufl.edu/~arunava/Teaching/cap6617sp21/Readings/frey2001factor.pdf) · [PWFA phase map](../decisions/ADR-118.md)

**4. An associative memory needs a basin around the target.** Hopfield’s model uses recurrent dynamics to retrieve a stored pattern from a nearby state. The *basin of attraction* is the set of starting states that flow to a particular attractor. A target can persist when presented cleanly and still have a basin too small to include a corrupted cue. An energy that decreases can also lead to an unintended stable state. Modern Hopfield networks use explicit stored-pattern similarities. Ramsauer and colleagues derive retrieval bounds that depend on pattern separation and the query’s location, and distinguish individual-pattern states from mixtures. Their update, storage access and computational budget differ from PWFA’s phase dynamics. For PWFA the relevant basin is operational: which corrupted cues produce the entire correct word under the anchored decoder by the fixed deadline? [Hopfield (1982)](https://www.dna.caltech.edu/courses/cs191/paperscs191/Hopfield82.pdf) · [Ramsauer et al. (ICLR 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)

**5. The oscillator literature identifies a specific stability problem.** Hölzel and Krischer showed that under their pattern assumptions a Hebbian Kuramoto network can have an attractive set containing several stored states, with small perturbations permitting switching. Li, Zhao and Zhou studied a related model with an additional second-harmonic interaction. For two-memory configurations they used the eigenvalues of the linearized dynamics to distinguish the stability of desired and competing binary patterns; their broader retrieval procedure uses that construction. In their bipolar encoding a word and its sign reversal are equivalent up to phase translation. PWFA scores a word and its complement as different targets relative to a spatial origin. PWFA also has a lagged structural interaction, spatial bumps and a finite readout time. These papers supply ways to analyze stability and basins; their error-free retrieval conclusions require their own encoding and equations. [Hölzel & Krischer (2015)](https://www.ph.nat.tum.de/fileadmin/w00bya/chemphys/Publications/Hoelzel_SIAM_2015.pdf) · [Li, Zhao & Zhou (2026), paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/sapm.70232) · [Open manuscript, Eq. 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12223)

## What the four PWFA words actually allow

Lean checked the six pairwise distances in file order: **16, 6, 4, 10, 12 and 6**. The minimum is four. The cue `?10?010011110001` has visible distances **2, 12, 6 and 2** from the four words. The first and fourth words can both generate that identical cue with exactly two flips and two erasures. A decoder given just that cue and library cannot tell which one was sent. On the actual sixteen scheduled cues the original target was always the unique nearest word; the existing lookup recovered 16/16. Both findings hold for the same library. [Words](../experiments/first-recall/words.txt) · [Existing lookup results](../experiments/first-trained-recall.md#diagnostic-results) · [Lean proof and executable](../../scripts/experiments/error-erasure-distance.lean)

The exhaustive calculation counts **43,680 target-labelled cases** ($4\binom{16}{2}\binom{14}{2}$). With ties scored as misses, nearest visible word gives **41,958 correct (96.06%)**, **1,422 ties** and **300 wrong unique answers**. There is a useful distinction about the noise model. When the decoder knows exactly two visible bits were flipped, only candidates at visible distance two can have generated that input. In 42,258 cases this identifies a unique target, including the 300 cases where ordinary nearest distance preferred a closer, incompatible word. The other 1,422 cases have multiple compatible targets. These are exact counts for this library and corruption rule, independent of oscillator dynamics. Uniform weighting over those cases gives even a perfect decoder only 3.94 percentage points of headroom over simple nearest lookup on this library. The project’s matched comparator and evaluation across libraries still require their own protocol. [Mission criterion](../decisions/ADR-100.md)

## What survives to the readout

On 2026-11-28 we applied the existing static Lean lookup to the archived *decoded* words from the held-training sweep. It received the four stored words and each 16-symbol output. The evaluator joined the returned identity to the hidden target. This retrospective test asks whether a single nearest-word correction at the end would have recovered the target. It adds access to the raw word library. PWFA's original exact-word score remains 0/16 at every strength. [Published raw outputs](../experiments/structural-coupling-at-retrieval.md#intermediate-strength-results) · [Lookup implementation](../../scripts/experiments/first-trained-recall.lean)

| Input to nearest-word lookup | Unique correct target | Equal-distance tie | Unique wrong target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original corrupted cue | 16 | 0 | 0 |
| Decoded at $t=40$, structural strength 0 | 16 | 0 | 0 |
| Strength ¼ | 10 | 5 | 1 |
| Strength ½ | 9 | 2 | 5 |
| Strength ¾ | 5 | 7 | 4 |
| Strength 1 | 4 | 7 | 5 |

The counts agree at both step sizes. At strength zero the thresholded output still identifies the intended stored word in every case. At full strength only four thresholded outputs do so uniquely under this distance rule. The continuous phase field could retain information discarded by thresholding. For reproduction, take columns 1 and 3 of any archived corrupted-cue prediction TSV and pass the resulting two-column file to `--visible-lookup` with the frozen library. Match its returned IDs against the schedule; a tied lookup abstains.

**Can the other positions identify a flipped bit?** In a second static check we temporarily hid position $j$ in the original visible cue, ran the same lookup, and kept only bit $j$ of its chosen word. We repeated this for all sixteen positions without giving the lookup any target labels. An ambiguous nearest word yielded an abstention. This recovered all 32 flipped bits, all 32 masked bits and 189/192 initially correct bits. The other three positions tied, all on cue `013` (positions 3, 7 and 10). That gives **15/16 exact words and one abstention**. The ordinary full-cue lookup gives 16/16. This deliberately harder check shows that the other positions contain evidence for reversing every scheduled flip. A model-side diagnostic would repeat the same omission with the oscillator supplying the answer at $t=40$. Its cost would be sixteen retrieval trajectories per cue and it would need separate reporting under the matched-budget objective. [Original inputs](../experiments/first-recall/schedule.tsv) · [Scoring denominator](../decisions/ADR-122.md)

The subsequent oscillator experiment tested the same omission using the held matrix as its only trained memory. The assembled readout recovered 8/16 whole words on the original library and 8/16 on a separately frozen fresh library. All successes on each roster came from the first word and its complement. ## Scope of the Lean check

On 2026-11-27, against `4cca557`, Codex (GPT-6), the author in the internal reviewer role, closed three findings for an experimental coding-theory check under ADR-109. Its inputs are binary words, visible positions and designated erasures. Its conclusions concern information available to a decoder.

- **EC-01.** Start from the pinned mathlib Hamming metric. Bound the loss of distance when erasing positions, apply the triangle inequality on the visible coordinates and use a strict separation inequality for unique nearest decoding. A tie must remain possible at the boundary.
- **EC-02.** Check the six distances between the actual four words and a concrete ambiguous cue with exactly two disjoint flips and two masks from either of two words. Compare these worst-case examples with the scores on the frozen roster.
- **EC-03.** Enumerate each target, unordered pair of masks and unordered pair of flips outside the masks exactly once. Separate a unique nearest correct word, a nearest-distance tie and a wrong unique nearest word. Also count how many target-labelled cases are compatible with multiple sources given the *exact* corruption counts. These counts describe an exhaustive static decoding check on one library; they do not estimate PWFA performance.

These remedies cover only the distance proof and finite examples. The phase solver, experiment inputs and recall policy retain the previously recorded experimental scope. [Research process](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#lean-experiments-for-research-decisions)

The [compiled check](../../scripts/experiments/error-erasure-distance.lean) uses mathlib's triangle inequality after replacing both erased symbols by the same filler. It bounds the loss of distance by the number of erasures. An executable part checks the published library, the ambiguous example and the full enumeration. Reproduce it from the repository root with:
