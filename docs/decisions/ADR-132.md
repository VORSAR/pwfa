# ADR-132 — Reviewer identity in internal review notes

Accepted on 2026-11-28. The owner selected option 1. The choice concerns the wording of a record, so there was nothing for Lean to evaluate. [Review policy](ADR-109.md) · [Question format](ADR-130.md) · [Lean rule and its common-sense case](ADR-131.md)

## Decision

Identify the reviewer in each new internal review note as **the assistant, acting as internal reviewer**, with the review date and the revision reviewed. Name no product or model. The note must still make clear that the review is internal and role-separated, and that no outside reviewer checked the work. Notes written before this decision keep their wording.

This amends one requirement of ADR-109, that the reviewing model remain visible. Every other requirement of ADR-109 stands: bounded scope, numbered findings, closure of every in-scope finding before implementation, truthful build evidence, a post-implementation check, and no claim of independence.

## Alternatives and context

ADR-109 requires that the review author and model stay visible in each record. The owner also requires that committed material name no AI product or model. The two could not both be met.

- **1. Say "the assistant".** The assistant recommended this option and the owner selected it. It uses the term ADR-109 already uses for the reviewer and keeps the record clear that neither a person nor an outside reviewer performed the review. The model is no longer recorded.
- **2. Name the model in review notes only.** This gives the most complete record and matches the earlier notes. It would make review notes an exception to the owner's naming rule.
- **3. Say "the author" only.** This is the strictest reading of the naming rule. A reader could take the reviewer for a person.
