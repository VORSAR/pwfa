# ADR-118 — Bit polarity and phase offsets for torus validation

Accepted on 2026-11-24. After the point-readout experiment passed, the owner selected option 1A. [Origin anchor](ADR-116.md) · [Research evidence](../research/bit-to-phase-map.md#checked-results-for-the-decision-discussion)

## Decision

Encode a presented 0 at a negative phase offset from the origin and a presented 1 at the corresponding positive offset. The primary alphabet is −90° and +90°. Use −45° and +45° as a comparison on the same validation cues. The sign convention applies to the signed-imaginary readout. Thresholds and treatment of low-confidence observations remain open. [Encoding contract](../mathematics/encoding-decoding.md) · [Recall benchmark](../experiments/benchmarks.md)

## Alternatives and evidence

- **1A. Primary ±90°, with a ±45° comparison.** The assistant recommended this option and the owner selected it. It combines the largest ideal sign margin with a test of gentler spatial stimulation.
- **1B. Initially use ±90° alone.** This requires fewer runs and leaves the sensitivity to stimulus angle open.
- **1C. Compare both as coequal candidates.** This gives comparable validation cost to 1A and defers selection of the primary alphabet until a separate validation set has been evaluated.

For a positive-magnitude point observation and zero threshold, the correct sign survives differential angular error strictly below 90° or 45°, respectively. Either symbol can reach a tie at its boundary. The next empirical check is the full sixteen-bit word at recall time on paired cues.
