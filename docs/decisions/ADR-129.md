# ADR-129 — Zero phase lag in learned retrieval

Accepted on 2026-11-27. The owner selected A after reviewing the two-site Lean calculation. This pilot choice applies to retrieval from both the 25-unit and the 50-unit training conditions. [Training durations](ADR-127.md) · [Two-site calculation](../research/recall-pilot-checks.md#two-site-phase-correction-at-t40)

## Decision

Use zero phase lag for the sine interaction driven by the learned field $L_i=N^{-1}\sum_j M_{ij}z_j$. At site $i$ its angular contribution before multiplication by a learned gain is $\operatorname{Im}(\overline{z_i} L_i)$. An aligned self-entry contributes zero torque. The structural term retains its own lag, $\alpha=\pi/2-0.1$ in the reference configuration. The pilot still needs a rule for the learned gain, its relation to the recovered scalar gain, and any feedback during training. [Learned field](ADR-128.md) · [Evolution contract](../mathematics/evolution.md) · [Historical scalar gain](ADR-093.md)

## Alternatives

- **A. Zero learned lag.** The owner accepted the recommended option. In an isolated pair trained to agree, this gives the fastest correction of the two tested lag values at equal positive gain and takes one retrieval run per cue and condition.
- **C. Compare both lags on the same cues.** This would measure the effect of the lag on actual recall and double the retrieval comparisons.
- **B. Use the historical structural lag for both channels.** This would use one angle throughout the dynamics. In the verified pair it yields about 9.98% of the zero-lag decay coefficient.

The control starts two phases a quarter-turn apart and compares their relative error at $t=40$. A four-word, 16-bit recall measurement still requires the complete trained dynamics and decoder.
