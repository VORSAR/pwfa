# ADR-126 — Use the full scheduled training episode

Accepted on 2026-11-25. The owner selected A for the first trained recall pilot after reviewing the Lean window comparison. [Time-average decision](ADR-125.md) · [Window comparison](../research/recall-pilot-checks.md#exact-time-averages-on-a-training-window)

## Decision

For each word, begin accumulating signed pair alignment at the scheduled start of its training episode and finish at the scheduled end. Divide the integral by that episode’s actual positive duration. Include early transients and later activity. Store the resulting summary once with the same weight as each other completed word. The physical episode duration and the cue’s onset, duration and release within the schedule remain to be specified. [Training transitions](../mathematics/memory.md#training-and-recall-transitions) · [Across-word weights](ADR-124.md)

## Alternatives

- **A. Full episode.** The owner accepted the assistant’s recommendation. All training moments contribute and the protocol needs only the two episode boundaries.
- **B. Final half only.** This emphasizes later behavior at the cost of discarding earlier observations.
- **C. Compare both windows on matched cues.** This would measure the effect of window choice on recall accuracy at the cost of a second training condition.

For the bounded signal $C(t)=t$ on $[-1,1]$, Lean evaluates the full-episode mean to zero and the final-half mean to +1/2. This illustrates the choice of training window. Trained recall at $t=40$ remains an empirical endpoint.
