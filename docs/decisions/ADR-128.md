# ADR-128 — Equal cell-area weights for learned retrieval

Accepted on 2026-11-25. The owner selected A after the finite-row Lean comparison. This decision applies to both training durations in the development pilot. [Duration comparison](ADR-127.md) · [Checked retrieval weights](../research/recall-pilot-checks.md#which-stored-pairs-reach-the-retrieval-field)

## Decision

For the uniform torus grid of $N>0$ sites, form the learned retrieval field at site $i$ from the stored signed pair matrix $M$ and the current unit phases $z$:

$$L_i=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} M_{ij}z_j.$$

Use every column, including the diagonal and sites outside the structural radius. Apply the cell area once. A signed row can sum to zero and still have a nonzero learned field. The structural kernel and its raw/normalized comparison have their own contract. The gain and phase lag applied to the learned field, and its role during training, remain open. [Structural quadrature](ADR-114.md) · [Signed training input](ADR-123.md)

## Alternatives

- **A. Equal cell area for every stored pair.** The owner accepted the assistant’s recommendation. This permits direct influence from distant learned associations using the dense matrix already being stored.
- **B. Multiply by the structural kernel.** The finite-range kernel would eliminate direct influence from distant pairs and could reduce retrieval work.
- **C. Compare both weightings.** This would test spatial weighting against exact-word recall with an additional retrieval condition at each training duration.

With $|M_{ij}|\le1$ and $|z_j|\le1$ the magnitude of $L_i$ is at most one. The Lean two-site example shows that the remote sign can affect the learned field and, conditional on the chosen coupling, the instantaneous phase speed. Its effect on trained recall at $t=40$ remains an empirical question. [Lean check](../../scripts/experiments/learned-spatial-weights.lean) · [Recall benchmark](../experiments/benchmarks.md)
