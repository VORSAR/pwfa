# ADR-131 — Every decision put to the owner is evaluated in Lean first

Accepted on 2026-11-28. The owner made it a standing rule that every decision coming to the owner is evaluated with a Lean program first. Asked what to do with choices that Lean cannot evaluate, the owner answered to use common sense in those cases. [Earlier practice](ADR-117.md) · [Question format](ADR-130.md) · [Project workflow](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#lean-experiments-for-research-decisions)

## Decision

Before a decision is put to the owner, evaluate it with a Lean program in the pinned environment. The evaluation covers what the options depend on: the assumptions that could change their ranking, the figures quoted for them, and any claim that an option cannot work. Keep the program in the repository, run it with the other research checks, and link it from the question or its supporting note. A figure quoted to the owner comes from such a program or is labelled as not yet reproduced in Lean.

Some choices have nothing a Lean program can evaluate, such as the wording of a record. Use common sense there. Bring the choice to the owner in the usual form, say plainly that nothing was checked in Lean and why, and do not invent a check for the sake of having one.

This widens ADR-117, which required a check where a proposal had a material, formalizable assumption. The internal review policy for experimental Lean is unchanged. [Review policy](ADR-109.md)

## First application

Three choices had been put to the owner earlier the same day. The options for the next recall experiment rested on a Lean check of the pair interaction at both bit alphabets. For the comparator, the nearest-word figures came from an existing Lean enumeration, while the memory-network figures came from a calculation outside the repository. Those figures must be reproduced in Lean before the comparator decision is recorded. The wording of review notes had nothing for Lean to evaluate.
