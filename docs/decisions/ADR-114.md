# ADR-114 — Compare raw and row-normalized structural quadrature

Accepted on 2026-11-24. The owner selected option B in the numerical architecture discussion. The scope is the structural coupling in the first unforced torus validation runs. [Periodic quadrature](ADR-098.md) · [Kernel contract](ADR-111.md) · [Grid sizes](ADR-112.md) · [Radius comparison](ADR-113.md)

## Decision

For a grid with $N$ total sites and a continuum kernel $G$, let $w_{ij}=G(x_i,x_j)/N$ and $M_i=\sum_j w_{ij}$. Evolve the same initial field under two coequal operators. The raw operator uses $w_{ij}$. The normalized operator uses $w_{ij}/M_i$. Report the raw mass and its signed error $M_i-1$ for both arms. Pair the results by kernel, physical radius, grid, mode, and time settings.

Both finite-range kernels include the center site, where their selected profiles are strictly positive for either radius. Thus $M_i>0$ for the structural rows. For the uniform control the raw and normalized weights both equal $1/N$ in exact arithmetic. One uniform control per grid supplies both references. The three grids and two radii give 24 finite-range configurations across the two operators, plus three uniform controls, before varying modes or time steps.

## Alternatives discussed

- **A. Simulate normalized rows and report raw mass error.** The assistant recommended this for equal total coupling at every resolution and a direct synchronized-rate check.
- **B. Evolve both operators.** The owner selected paired evolutions to measure how row normalization changes wave rates. Both arms contribute to the validation results.
- **C. Initially use only raw weights.** This follows direct sampling of the analytic kernel; coarse-grid rate error combines mass error with differences in the spatial weighting.

## Validation implications

For synchronized data the expected instantaneous rate in the raw arm is $\omega-\kappa M_i\sin\alpha$ and in the normalized arm it is $\omega-\kappa\sin\alpha$. Translation invariance on these uniform periodic grids makes the theoretical row mass independent of the center. Compare measured rates with the corresponding finite-grid prediction and with the continuum reference. Report both grid dimensions for the axial and diagonal waves. These are predictions for validating a future solver. The choice concerns structural rows; the learned pairwise update and observation-row contract have their own specifications.
