# ADR-111: Finite-range structural coupling on the flat torus

- **Status:** Accepted for the written baseline. Mathematical review is pending.
- **Decision date:** 2026-11-23
- **Selected option:** 2A
- **Provenance:** The owner selected 2A in the PWFA design discussion after reviewing the alternatives below.
- **Affects:** [Flat torus oscillator baseline](../verification/slices/torus-oscillator-baseline.md#finite-range-and-comparison-kernels)

## Context

The recovered ring sweep varies cosine contrast over a kernel with global support. The 2D baseline uses radial weights on the approved periodic Euclidean metric. Its design choice is whether the primary coupling has finite range or retains a global background. [Historical ring reference](../mathematics/ring-oscillator-reference.md) · [Periodic Euclidean metric](ADR-105.md)

## Alternatives presented at decision time

- **2A — Finite-range smooth kernel.** High alignment and high feasibility. Adopt the draft's normalized smooth bump with configurable radius, a same-radius disk comparator, and a separate uniform control. The historical $a$-sweep stays on the ring.
- **2B — Mix local and global coupling.** Medium alignment and high feasibility. The family $(1-a)+aG_R^{\mathrm{smooth}}$ gives uniform coupling at $a=0$ and a positive background weight wherever $a<1$. At $a=0.8$, every pair receives global background weight $0.2$.

## Owner decision

Use the [normalized smooth radial bump](../verification/slices/torus-oscillator-baseline.md#smooth-primary-kernel) with support radius $0<R<\tfrac12$ as the primary 2D structural kernel. Use a normalized disk at the same radius as the profile comparator and a uniform all-to-all kernel as a separate control. The radius stays configurable. A nominal numerical value and sweep belong to the experimental protocol.

## Recommendation and consequences

The assistant recommended 2A because it puts finite-range coupling in the primary model and retains a direct uniform control at the same total coupling strength. The smooth and disk kernels differ in the distribution of weight within the shared radius as well as in regularity at its edge. Any observed difference between them is a comparison of profiles.

## Proof and test obligations

Check nonnegative unit mass, the support boundary, smoothness at zero separation and at the cut locus, and equal rotation rates under sign reversal and axis exchange. Verify that the distance in the kernel is the named Euclidean torus distance. [Written metric remediation](../verification/slices/spatial-metric-remediation.md)

The published disk geometry and its scope are indexed as EXT-004. The earlier Batch 4 record remains a provenance question. This dated owner choice establishes the current 2D kernel.
